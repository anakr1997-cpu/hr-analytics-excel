#  HR Analytics & Employee Turnover Analysis

## Project Overview

An Excel-based HR analytics project analyzing employee turnover, salary distribution, workload patterns, and employee characteristics across departments.

The analysis uses employee-level HR data to explore patterns in turnover, satisfaction, performance, working hours, projects, and salary levels.

**Tool:** Microsoft Excel

---

##  Business Questions

- What is the overall employee turnover rate?
- How does turnover vary across departments?
- How are salary levels distributed across departments?
- Which departments have a higher proportion of employees working unusually high hours?
- How do employee satisfaction, evaluations, projects, and working hours vary across departments?

---

##  Analysis & Key Findings

### Employee Turnover

The dataset contains **14,999 employee records**, including **3,571 employees who left the company**.

This represents an overall turnover rate of approximately **23.8%**.

Turnover varied across departments. In this dataset:

- **HR** had the highest turnover rate at approximately **29.1%**.
- **Accounting** followed at approximately **26.6%**.
- **Technical** was approximately **25.6%**.
- **Sales** had the largest number of employees who left, with **1,014 departures**, while its turnover rate was approximately **24.5%**.

The analysis compares both employee counts and turnover percentages to provide a more complete view of departmental turnover.

---

### Salary Distribution

Salary levels were analyzed across departments.

Across the overall employee population:

- **Low salary:** approximately 48.8%
- **Medium salary:** approximately 43.0%
- **High salary:** approximately 8.3%

Salary distributions varied by department.

The management department had a substantially larger proportion of employees in the high-salary category compared with most other departments.

---

### Workload & Outlier Hours

A percentile-based approach was used to identify employees with unusually high monthly working hours.

Employees whose monthly hours were above the **90th percentile** were classified as having outlier hours.

The analysis identified **1,451 employees**, representing approximately **9.7%** of the workforce.

The percentage varied by department. For example:

- **Technical:** approximately 10.6%
- **Sales:** approximately 10.3%
- **Accounting:** approximately 10.2%
- **IT:** approximately 10.0%

This analysis provides an additional way to compare workload patterns across departments.

---

### Employee Characteristics

The analysis also compared departments using several employee-level metrics.

Overall dataset averages included:

| Metric | Overall Average |
|---|---:|
| Monthly Hours | 201.05 |
| Evaluation | 0.716 |
| Number of Projects | 3.80 |
| Satisfaction | 0.613 |

Median values were also calculated for monthly hours and satisfaction to provide additional context beyond averages.

##  Excel Analysis

The workbook includes several analysis worksheets:

### `HR_file`

Contains the employee-level HR dataset and the calculated **Outlier Hours** field.

### `Pivot`

Contains department-level analysis including:

- Employee counts
- Employees who left
- Average monthly hours
- Median monthly hours
- Average evaluation
- Average projects
- Average satisfaction
- Median satisfaction

### `Pivot2`

Analyzes salary distribution by department.

### `Sheet5`

Analyzes employees classified as having unusually high working hours.

### `Visuals`

Contains supporting calculations and visualizations used to communicate the analysis.
